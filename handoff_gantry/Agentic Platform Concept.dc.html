01 — Positioning

Everyone is shipping the agent. Nobody is shipping the harness.

Agent = model + harness. The market has spent two years on the model side and on the demo side. The harness — the part that decides whether an action is permitted, records that it happened, and can prove it to someone who was not in the room — is still assembled by hand at every company that needs it, and assembled badly.

The rubric's own arithmetic explains why this is a product and not a blog post. The overall level is the minimum across primitives, not the average. Nine strong layers and one absent trust layer is a level 1 system. That is a devastating rule for anyone assembling a harness piecemeal, because the pieces nobody builds are always 10, 11 and 12 — verification, observability, governance. They are unglamorous, they gate nothing anyone wants gated, and they are exactly what turns into an audit finding.

So the offer is narrow and hard to argue with: install one thing, and your floor stops being your weakest layer.

The one-line pitch

The open reference harness for agentic engineering. Security and evidence are the substrate, not a module.

◆Every model call and tool call passes one chokepoint, so observability is not a feature anyone can forget to turn on.
◆Authority is declared in version control and enforced at the boundary, not written in a policy nobody checks.
◆The installation scores itself against the rubric continuously, and links every score to evidence an auditor can read without an engineer present.

03 — The mechanism

The trust budget, and strictness as a choice.

You asked to explore the human-in-the-loop model. There are three obvious shapes, each incomplete on its own. The proposal is to collapse them into one mechanism rather than ship three features.

Option A · policy gates

Classes of action require approval, declared as code. Precise and auditable. Static — it cannot express an agent that has earned latitude, so teams route around it.

Option B · autonomy ladder

Per capability: human-led, human-assisted, fully autonomous. Legible to non-engineers, which is why the screenshots lead with it. Theatre unless promotion is tied to real sensor history.

Option C · review and rollback

Let it run, review after, revert. Fastest loop and correct for reversible work. Worthless the moment an action leaves the building — a payment, a filing, a production deploy.

Proposal

Every capability holds a rung. The rung decides where the human stands. Sensors decide the rung.

Rung sets the gate

Human-led means the gate is pre-action and blocking. Assisted means pre-action for the irreversible subset only. Autonomous means post-action review with rollback. One field, three behaviours — A and C stop being separate systems.

Promotion is earned

A capability rises a rung only after N runs at the current rung with zero sensor failures and zero human overrides. Promotion is a signed ledger event with a named approver. Demotion is automatic on failure and needs no meeting.

Why this is the soul of it

It is anchor 5 made operational. A failure does not just get fixed, it moves a rung and adds a sensor. The harness gets tighter where it was wrong and looser where it has been right, and both movements leave a record.

Profiles, so strictness is a choice that cannot lie.

Two things need to be selectable: how hard the sandbox is, and where the human stands. Making each a separate flag produces a matrix nobody understands. Instead one profile sets isolation, gate placement, anchoring and identity together, and the profile is a single argument at start-up. The rung defaults it hands you stay overridable per capability.

The design constraint that makes this safe: the conformance scorer reads what is actually running, never the profile name. Choosing the laptop profile visibly lowers primitive 05 and caps the overall level. Nothing is weakened silently, and a screenshot of a score always carries the profile that produced it.

laptop
Default
$ gantry up

One container, SQLite, local ledger file, local accounts. OCI isolation with a seccomp profile and an empty egress allowlist — real containment, no nested virtualisation, works on any machine. Rungs default to autonomous with post-hoc review, because on a laptop the work is reversible and the loop should be fast.

Honest ceiling — primitive 05 scores 3, so the overall level caps at 3. Stated on the dashboard, not buried.

team
Shared
$ gantry up --profile team

Kernel-level sandboxing, Postgres, OIDC approvers, tree heads anchored daily to object storage. Rungs default to assisted: pre-action gates on the irreversible subset only. The point where approvals start carrying a real identity from the directory.

regulated
Air-gapped
$ gantry up --profile regulated

MicroVM isolation, signing keys in an HSM or TPM, OIDC required with no local-account fallback, external timestamping, and no promotion above assisted without a named approver. Refuses to start when any of those are unavailable rather than degrading quietly.

Needs nested virtualisation. That requirement is the price of a 4 on primitive 05, and should be sold as such rather than apologised for.

04 — The audit layer

Do not build this on a blockchain.

You asked to be argued out of it, so: an auditor wants four things from a record. Integrity — nothing was altered. Attribution — who or what did it, under whose authority. Time — when, provably. Completeness — nothing was omitted.

A hash-chained log with per-entry signatures from a key held in a TPM or HSM delivers integrity, attribution and time. Completeness is a process control — it comes from the architecture forcing every call through one chokepoint, and no ledger design can supply it.

What a blockchain adds beyond that is distributed consensus, which solves exactly one problem: mutual distrust between parties who cannot agree on who operates the log. In a single-tenant install the operator is the client. There is no counterparty. Consensus buys nothing and charges throughput, key ceremony, operational surface, and — fatally for your air-gap requirement — peers.

There is also a credibility cost. Say "blockchain" to a pharma QA lead or a bank's model risk group and you are now the tenth vendor to say it. Say "transparency log, the same construction that secures certificate issuance on the public web" and you are speaking a language their PKI team already trusts.

Build this instead

An append-only Merkle transparency log

◆RFC 6962 construction. Signed tree heads, inclusion proofs, consistency proofs.
◆An auditor can verify a single event without reading, or being trusted with, the rest of the log.
◆Verification is an offline binary. No network, no node, no chain to sync.
◆Anchoring is a pluggable one-liner: WORM object store, an RFC 3161 timestamp authority, a client's internal notary — or a public chain, if a client insists on the word.

Net effect — you keep blockchain as a checkbox at the edge of the system and off the critical path. The architecture never depends on it, and it never has to be explained.

05 — Stack

The core language and the UI language are two different decisions.

Treating them as one is how Rust-versus-Blazor becomes a false choice. The control plane sits in the hot path of every model call, holds keys, and supervises sandboxes. The UI reads an API. Judge them separately.

Rust
Recommended · core

A single static binary, a container measured in megabytes, no runtime to install in an air-gapped environment. Memory safety is not a preference in a component whose job is to gate privileged actions — it is the difference between a security product and a security incident. Mature crypto and TLS, and real libraries for namespaces, seccomp and eBPF, which is where primitive 05 actually lives.

Cost — a smaller contributor pool for an OSS-first project, and a slower first quarter. Both are real. Neither is fatal if the plugin surface is language-agnostic, which it is.

Go
The honest alternative

Most of Rust's deployment story, a far larger contributor pool, and — the strongest argument — the entire cloud-native ecosystem you would otherwise reimplement is already Go-native. Policy engines, container runtimes, telemetry, transparency logs. You would be assembling rather than authoring.

Cost — a garbage collector in the hot path, and a weaker claim when a CISO asks why they should trust the gatekeeper. Choose this if velocity matters more than the assurance signal.

C# / Blazor
Not the core

A genuinely good language, and in a .NET bank or pharma it is a sales advantage — the client's own team can read, audit and extend it. That advantage is real and worth capturing. But it belongs in a first-class C# SDK, not in the core.

Cost as core — the whole .NET runtime enters the container, the agentic and cloud-native ecosystem is somewhere else entirely, and an OSS-first project becomes an island. Blazor Server also wants a persistent socket, which is awkward for an air-gapped appliance UI.

Verdict

Rust control plane shipped as one binary. The UI is a static web app that binary serves — no separate process, no node in the container. Agnosticism lives in the SDKs, and there you go wide: Python and TypeScript first because that is where agent authors are, then C# and Go. A .NET shop that wants a Blazor console builds it against the same API you did, which is the correct proof that the API is the product.

06 — Agnosticism

Six axes, and where each one is actually held.

You marked all six non-negotiable. Agnosticism is cheap to claim and expensive to keep, so each axis needs a named seam and a conformance test that runs in CI. Otherwise it decays into "works with Anthropic, compiles with the others."

Model provider

One gateway, provider adapters behind it. Anthropic, OpenAI, Bedrock, Azure, vLLM, Ollama. The seam is a normalised call event, so a trace looks identical whichever model produced it. Conformance test: the same run, three providers, three byte-comparable ledger shapes.

Air-gapped operation

The hardest constraint, and the one that disciplines everything else. No phone-home, no hosted control plane, no licence check, no CDN font. Verification is an offline binary. Test: the full suite runs with the network namespace empty.

Cloud or bare metal

Compose for a laptop, a Helm chart for a cluster, the same binary in both. Storage and secrets sit behind interfaces with a filesystem-and-SQLite default, so nothing requires a managed service to start.

Target tech stack

Sensors are declared, not built in: a sensor is a container plus a contract that emits a verdict and a fix-naming message. Whether it is pytest, cargo clippy, ArchUnit or a bespoke linter is the workload's business, not the platform's.

Agent framework

Two integration depths. Shallow: point any harness at the gateway and the tool broker and you inherit 04, 05, 11 and 12 for free. Deep: use the orchestrator and state store and you get 06 through 10. Claude Code, Copilot, LangGraph or a shell script all work at the shallow depth on day one.

Identity provider

OIDC for human approvers, so approvals carry a real identity from the client's directory. Local accounts exist for the air-gapped and single-developer cases. An approval without a resolvable identity is not an approval, and the ledger records which kind it was.

07 — Plan

Nine slices. Each one ends in a proof, not a demo.

Design, code, test, proof, next — as you asked. Two rules shape the order. Evidence cannot be retrofitted, so the ledger is slice one. And every slice must make the following slice safer to build, which is why the sandbox lands before the orchestrator.

A proof is adversarial and written down. Not "here is the feature working" but "here is the attack, and here is the record of it failing." Each slice commits a docs/proof/NN.md with the evidence and the conformance delta.

The proving workload

A sandboxed repository security audit. A small crew of agents checks out a repo, runs with no network route and no write access, and emits a signed findings report where every finding carries an inclusion proof back to the calls that produced it.

It was chosen because it exercises the layers nobody builds — 04, 05, 10, 11 and 12 all under real load — rather than the ones that demo well. The agents read untrusted third-party code, which is the honest test of the sandbox. Findings must be reproducible, which is the honest test of verification. And the output is a deliverable someone would actually pay for, so the proof and the marketing are the same artifact.

00
Schemas before code

The event schema and the policy schema. Everything downstream is a consumer of these two, and changing them later is the one migration you cannot afford.

Proof

A hand-written trace of one real agent run, expressed entirely in the schema, that a stranger can read and reconstruct without the author present.

01
Evidence ledger

Append-only Merkle log, signed tree heads, inclusion and consistency proofs, offline verifier. Primitive 11 exists before anything can fail to be recorded.

Proof

Edit one byte of one historical record. The verifier names the entry and the divergence point. Then verify a single event's inclusion offline, given nothing but the event and a signed head.

02
Model gateway

Provider adapters, normalised call events, window accounting, prompt and instruction versions pinned per call. The chokepoint that makes 02, 03 and 11 structural.

Proof

One agent run against a frontier API, a cloud-hosted model and a local model on a machine with no route to the internet. Three ledgers, one shape. Diff them in the doc.

03
Tool broker and policy engine

MCP as the wire protocol. A schema registry that refuses loose schemas, output tainting, and authority-as-code evaluated on every call.

Proof

A genuinely destructive command is denied. The ledger names the rule that fired, the version of the policy, and the identity the agent was acting as. Then try to publish a tool declared as "run any shell command" and watch the registry reject it.

04
Sandbox, credential broker, egress control

Per-run isolation, an allowlist enforced at the network namespace, and secrets the model never sees — it holds a handle, the broker injects the real value at the boundary.

Proof

Ship a deliberately hostile tool that reads every environment variable and posts them to an outside host, plus a prompt-injected document instructing the agent to help it. Both fail. Both are in the ledger. This is the launch demo.

05
Sensor bus

Register a sensor with a lifecycle placement — pre-integration, post-integration, continuous drift. Computational and inferential. Verdict messages are schema'd to name the fix, because an agent is the reader.

Proof

A failing sensor blocks the result. The agent reads the message, corrects on rerun, and passes — with no human in the loop and both attempts recorded. Then break the sensor itself and confirm a sensor that cannot fail is reported as broken, not as clean.

06
Orchestrator and trust budget

Durable workflow, lifecycle hooks, retries, blocking gates, OIDC approvals, and rungs with sensor-driven promotion and automatic demotion.

Proof

A capability earns its way from assisted to autonomous on real sensor history, promoted by a named approver, and is demoted automatically by the next failure. Read the whole arc back out of the ledger as a story.

07
Durable state and corpus graph

Plans, checkpoints and memory outside the model's attention. A persisted graph over the codebase and documents that the agent traverses instead of re-reading, with staleness expiry.

Proof

Kill the container mid-run. Resume. Nothing is lost and the ledger shows the seam. Separately: same task, graph retrieval versus flat retrieval, with the token and accuracy delta published including the cases where the graph lost.

08
Conformance scorer and the console

The rubric as a running service. Twelve live scores computed from real telemetry, N/A where the workload never exercises a layer, overall level as the minimum, every score linked to its evidence.

Proof

Score the platform with itself and publish the result, low numbers included, in the launch README. The worked example in the rubric is the template: three layers at 4 and an overall level of 2 is a more persuasive artifact than a clean sheet, and it cannot be faked.

09
Skills, sub-agents, templates

Signed skill packages with a resolver, delegation with narrowed scope, and harness templates for common service topologies. Last, because these are the layers whose value depends on everything below already holding.

Proof

Break a skill's metadata deliberately and confirm the resolver refuses to publish it rather than silently falling back to its title. Then delete a step a skill references and confirm something fails before a run does.

08 — Name

Four candidates. One recommendation.

The test for an OSS name that catches on: one or two syllables, works as a verb in a terminal, and carries a metaphor an engineer gets without explanation.

Gantry
Pick this
$ gantry up

The structure that holds something powerful and dangerous steady, gives engineers access to every part of it, and is deliberately left behind at ignition. That is a harness, exactly. Two syllables, verbs cleanly, and the metaphor needs no explanation. Repo Mariano215/gantry, binary gantry. One caveat worth checking before you commit: Gantry is also a long-running Joomla template framework, so search results will be muddy. Titling the README Gantry — the reference harness for agentic engineering separates them quickly. No domain is needed to launch; the repo is the front door.

Keel
$ keel init

The structural spine, and laying the keel is the first act of building a ship. Shortest of the four and the calmest. Weaker on the safety and access idea, stronger on foundation.

Ratchet
$ ratchet score

Turns one way only. A direct encoding of the compounding anchor and of automatic demotion. The most conceptually precise name here, and the least warm.

Assay
$ assay verify

To test a metal and certify what it actually is. Perfect for the evidence and conformance half, misleading about the runtime half. Better as the name of the scorer than the platform.

09 — Boundaries

What this is not, so the scope survives contact.

Not an agent framework

No opinion on how you write the loop. If it can call a model and a tool, it can run here.

Not a chat product

The console is for operators and auditors. There is no assistant UI, and adding one would cost the project its identity.

Not an eval platform

Evals are one kind of sensor and plug into the bus. Building a benchmark suite is a different company.

Not a skills marketplace

A registry with signing and resolution, yes. Curation, discovery and a hundred-and-thirty-seven-item catalogue, no. That is the thing the screenshots already are.

Not a compliance product

It produces the evidence a regime needs. It does not claim certification, and mapping evidence to a specific regime is consulting work — which is the business model, not the repo.

Not a model

Nothing here trains or serves weights. The whole thesis is that the harness is the part worth building.

10 — Settled

Decisions taken, and the two still open.

Recorded here so the plan has a stated basis and anyone joining later can see what was decided rather than inferring it.

Governance in the open repo

Evidence mappings ship in the repo — the control crosswalks, and which events satisfy which control. Interpretation and attestation stay as the service, because a crosswalk is unusable without someone who knows the auditor. Publishing the mappings is also what makes the project spread, and the mappings are the part that ages fastest anyway.

Sandbox floor

The laptop profile is the default and needs no nested virtualisation. Isolation is a real OCI sandbox with seccomp and an empty egress allowlist, not an honour system — it simply scores 3 rather than 4, and says so.

Trust budget selectability

Rung defaults come from the profile and are overridable per capability. What is not overridable is the record: a chosen rung, an override and a promotion are all ledger events with an author.

Scorer

Built in, and the scoring rules ship as data. A third party can re-run them against an exported ledger and reach the same twelve numbers without trusting the binary that produced them. Without that, a self-scoring platform is marketing.

Attribution

Both sources credited in the README above the architecture — the twelve-primitive decomposition, and Böckeler’s guide/sensor taxonomy, published April 2026. Borrowing openly reads as confidence.

Still open · name collision

Gantry needs a five-minute check against crates.io, npm and the Joomla project’s search footprint before the repo is created. Keel and Ratchet remain the fallbacks and both survive the same test more easily.

Still open · audit crew scope

The proving workload needs a boundary or it becomes a security product. My proposal: secrets, dependency provenance and authorisation-boundary findings only. No SAST engine, no rule authoring, no dashboard. It exists to prove the harness, and the day it starts competing with Semgrep the project has lost its subject.

The onboarding wedge

Nobody installs a control plane. They will run a scan on their own repo and argue with the score.

$ gantry scan

Reads what is already there — instruction files, hooks, test gates, declared permissions, MCP config, CI — and scores twelve primitives with a file path or an explicit "looked in X and Y, found nothing" behind every number. Read-only, sixty seconds, no account. This is the artifact people post.

$ gantry apply

Scaffolds the missing layers as a reviewable diff, never a silent write. Hooks, instruction pack, sensor registrations, declared authority. Ranked by the rubric's own remediation order, so the first thing it offers is the thing with the largest expected gain.

$ gantry up

Brings up the ledger, broker and sandbox on the laptop profile. From here the scan stops being a snapshot and becomes live telemetry — the same twelve numbers, now computed from real runs instead of from configuration.

$ gantry verify

Checks the ledger offline and re-runs the published scoring rules against it. Anyone can run this against an exported ledger they were handed, which is what keeps a self-scoring platform honest.

One thing to be honest about

A hook is an enforcement point, not a security boundary. A user who edits their own settings can remove it, so hooks alone cannot carry primitive 05 — that is what the sandbox and the egress namespace are for. What hooks can do is make removal visible: the settings hash goes into every run's opening event, so a harness that was switched off leaves a gap in the record with a timestamp on it. Detection rather than prevention, and labelled as such in the score.

Build it with the thing it harnesses

Point Gantry at its own repo the week slice 01 can run at all. Every proof document then gets written from a real ledger instead of assembled by hand, and the launch score is a byproduct of construction rather than an exercise. The rubric's worked example already shows the shape of it: the CI added to catch mistakes caught its author's mistakes within minutes of existing. That is the note to launch on.
